BEFORE QUOTING THE "MONOTONE (descending)" LINE ABOVE - a caution about this script's own output.

That line fires on the ORDER OF THE MEANS alone and says "consistent with a real dial". Here it is
printed directly beneath "COLLAPSED at n=9", which is the line that actually matters, and the two
read as if they disagree. They do not. Every range overlaps every other range, so the ordering of
the means carries no information: with a 1.79x within-condition spread and a 1.14x gap between the
widest-separated pair, three means will land in SOME order whatever the field does, and one of the
six possible orders is "descending". Getting it is a 1-in-6 coincidence, not evidence.

The monotonicity test is only meaningful once the endpoints separate. That check should have been
gated on the separation result rather than printed unconditionally, and it is a fair example of the
thing this whole series keeps re-learning: a statistic that is computed whether or not it is
applicable will eventually be quoted in a context where it is not.

THE VERDICT IS THE OVERLAP. No spelling and no tier of `reasoning.effort` / `reasoning_effort`
changed how much this model thinks on this path, at nine runs per condition.
