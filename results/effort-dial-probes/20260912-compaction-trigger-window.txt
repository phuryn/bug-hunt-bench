Is the compaction trigger a fixed size, or a fraction of the window? - 2026-09-12

VERDICT (written by hand; the script's automatic one said NO VERDICT and was too strict - see the
note at the bottom): A FRACTION OF THE WINDOW, and close to 30% of it in both conditions.

    200,000-token model    compacts at ~62,000       (47,677 - 66,011 across four triggers)
    1,000,000-token model  compacts at ~300,000      (292,063 - 312,465 across five triggers)

The 1M half is decisive on its own: five compactions inside a 20-point range on one run, on the same
fixture, and the run finished clean at 66 turns. A five-fold window buys a five-fold budget. Nothing
about a fixed trigger size survives that.

Same job in both conditions: read the files with the Read tool, in order, whole,
nothing else. Same fixture, same machine, sequential, first-party endpoint.

WHY THIS WAS RUN. The breaker that killed two arms of the Sep 12 wave was traced to the
harness compacting them at roughly a fifth of the context the 1M arms get. The supporting
1M numbers were bench arms - different models, repos and prompts - and the 200K number was a
controlled job, so the comparison was between two different kinds of evidence. This makes it
one kind. --autocompact is not used in either condition, and on this route it would not matter if it
were: it is inert first-party (20260912-autocompact-flag.txt) and only live through a shim. So the
only thing separating these two runs is the model.

WHERE THE 200K NUMBER COMES FROM, SAID OUT LOUD. The 200K run HERE did not compact - Haiku read three
of the six files, answered, and ended at a context of 63,479, just short of a trigger. The four 200K
triggers quoted in the verdict are from the two runs of the SAME fixture in
20260912-autocompact-flag.txt, which did keep reading. Borrowing them is legitimate only because
--autocompact is inert on this route, which is exactly what that receipt's controlled pair
establishes - its two runs declared 100,000 and 1,000,000 and triggered in the same place. If that
pair is ever overturned, this verdict loses its 200K half and keeps its 1M half.

COST. The 1M condition is not a cheap probe: 60 Read calls and 66 turns of a 1M-context model came
to $8.40. It is recorded because this is the number the whole breaker investigation rests on, and
because a reader deciding whether to repeat it should know the price before starting.
