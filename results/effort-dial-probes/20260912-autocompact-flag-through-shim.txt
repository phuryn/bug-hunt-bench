Is --autocompact inert through a shim too? - 2026-09-12

VERDICT: INERT HERE TOO, and it overturns a claim published a few hours ago. First triggers 89,061
against 86,706 across a tenfold difference in the declared window, and all six triggers across the
two runs sit inside 86,059-89,061. The flag is inert on BOTH routes. The arm-level reading that said
otherwise - gpt-oss-20b at 52,023/57,418 unflagged and 24,514 flagged, four hours apart - was a
natural experiment with more than one variable in it, and this controlled pair beats it.

WHAT THAT LEAVES UNEXPLAINED, SAID PLAINLY RATHER THAN PAPERED OVER. The same upstream model, the
same shim, the same flag and the same host compacted at 23,863-26,498 inside the bench arm and at
86,059-89,061 here. Six triggers in each case, both clusters tight, a 3.4x gap between them. So
something about the ARM configuration - not the flag, not the route, not the model - moves the point
at which this harness compacts, and nothing measured today says what. `pre_tokens` is read after a
turn completes, so part of any single gap is just the size of the turn that crossed the line; a 3.4x
gap between two tight six-sample clusters is not that.

WHAT SURVIVES. The first-party pair (Haiku 4.5 at ~62,000, Sonnet 5 five times between 292,063 and
312,465, same fixture) still shows the trigger scaling with the model's real context window. The
breaker is still reproducible on Anthropic's own route. And the two held arms still have no
configuration worth re-running - if anything more firmly, because the one lever that might have given
them a bigger budget does nothing on either route.
